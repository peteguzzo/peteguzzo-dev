Every so often you meet someone who genuinely believes they don't need video content for their business. Not because they've thought it through — because they haven't. The reasons usually sound like this:

**"Everyone's doing it, so it's crowded."** YouTube had over a billion mobile video views per day at the time this was written. Being in a crowded space is not the same as being invisible in it. Being bad is what makes you invisible.

**"My audience doesn't watch video."** Your audience has a smartphone. Everyone's audience watches video.

**"It's too expensive."** The range of what video costs is enormous. A well-produced thirty-second spot is not the same budget conversation as a series of short social clips. Knowing what your goal is before the production conversation determines what you actually need to spend.

**"I don't know how to measure it."** View counts are a starting metric, not the only one. Completion rates, click-through from video to conversion, brand search lift after a campaign — these are all measurable. Not measuring doesn't mean it's not working.

**"It won't look professional."** If you hire the right people, it will. If you're using your phone and a ring light with no production planning, this concern is valid and the solution is to hire someone who knows what they're doing.

**"We tried it and it didn't work."** What was the content? Where did it run? What did it ask people to do? "We made a video once" is not the same as a video content strategy.

**"We'll do it next quarter."** You've been saying this.

**"Our competitors aren't doing it."** That's not a reason not to — that's a gap.

**"We don't have time."** Production is a managed process. You don't have to run it yourself.

**"I just don't believe in it."** That's not a strategy. That's a preference.

The businesses that wait for certainty before investing in video are watching the businesses that didn't wait build the audience they wanted.
