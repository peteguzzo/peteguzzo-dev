Tampa Bay wasn't on the production map ten years ago.

It is now.

The question is why it changed — and what it means if you're trying to produce something here.

## What changed

The short version: the facilities caught up.

For a long time, Tampa Bay had location and talent. Weather, architecture, coastline, a downtown that's been growing. But the infrastructure gap was real. Companies producing national-level work flew crews in from markets that had the facilities, the equipment, and the deep crew base to support serious production.

That gap has closed significantly.

Shear Media Studios added approximately 15,000 square feet of purpose-built production infrastructure to the St. Petersburg side of the market — soundstages, a broadcast control room, podcast and influencer suites, live-commerce environments. Other facilities have expanded. Crew depth in Tampa Bay has grown as the market has matured.

The result is a market that can now support productions that would have required Miami, Atlanta, or New York five years ago.

## What the market can actually handle now

**National commercial production.** Crew of 80+ on a single campaign. Multiple jurisdictions if the work calls for it.

**Live broadcast.** Multi-stream productions with real transmission infrastructure — including recurring broadcast partnerships with national media companies operating out of Shear Media Studios.

**Podcast and influencer production.** Multiple professional environments, recurring-production-ready, in a single facility.

**Corporate multi-stream events.** Multi-camera, multi-destination, run from one control room.

**Documentary and branded content.** Location variety, coast to coast geography compressed into one market. Crew infrastructure to support longer shoots.

That's a starting point, not a complete list. But it's enough to know that Tampa Bay is a market where serious work can happen.

## Why location matters beyond the weather

Tampa Bay has become a real lifestyle market. Companies thinking about where their teams want to work, travel, and stay for a week of production are increasingly looking at Tampa Bay alongside more established markets.

That's a recruiting argument, a retention argument, and — for the right production — a location argument rolled into one.

## What still requires planning

Crew depth is better than it was. It's not New York or LA.

If you're bringing a production to Tampa Bay, plan your crew in advance. The A-list talent in this market does book. Productions that rely on finding the right people at the last minute still run into problems.

And facility availability follows the same logic. The studios worth using stay booked — especially for recurring productions, locking time early is not optional.

But those are scheduling problems, not capability problems. The capability is here. The scheduling just has to happen earlier than people in other markets are used to.
