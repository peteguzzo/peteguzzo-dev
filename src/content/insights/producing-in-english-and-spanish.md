Spanglish. It's not just a funny word — it's a shorthand for how Kestum Bilt approaches production for brands that need to reach both English and Spanish-speaking audiences at the same time.

Most production companies treat the Hispanic market as a translation job. You shoot in English, you hand the script to a translator, you dub it or subtitle it, and you call it multicultural marketing. That's not what this is.

## Two Markets. One Production.

Simultaneous bilingual production means the approach, the casting, the script, and the execution are designed from the start to work in both languages — not adapted after the fact. The result is content that actually feels native to each audience, not like a dubbed version of something that was never made for them.

We've been doing this for clients across 30+ Hispanic-market projects over the years. The efficiency is real: one production day, two deliverable sets, none of the awkwardness that comes from a post-production translation workflow.

## Why It Matters

The U.S. Hispanic market is not a niche. It's one of the largest consumer segments in the country, and it's one that most production companies — especially outside major markets — haven't figured out how to serve authentically.

Brands that want to reach that audience have two options: find a specialty shop that only does Spanish-language work, or find a company that can run both simultaneously without treating either market as secondary.
