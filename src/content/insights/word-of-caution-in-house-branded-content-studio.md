We love branded content. We've been creating a lot of it for clients, and we've also watched more of it being created in-house by branded content studios — because the demand to produce at the speed of social media doesn't stop.

Maybe you're getting ready to build one. Here's what to think about first.

## The Volume Trap

Content marketing calendars demand two to three posts a week. That's a significant production burden. When margins are thin, the natural solution is to staff your studio with people who "get social media" and hand them an iPhone.

The problem: most of what gets produced this way is disposable. And if breaking through the clutter is the actual goal, disposable content is working against you.

## Two Things Worth Knowing Before You Build

**First — define what "quality" means for your brand before you hire anyone.** Not production value in the abstract, but specifically: what level of execution reflects well on your brand versus what level actively undermines it? That threshold is different for a B2B software company than it is for a consumer lifestyle brand. Know yours before you start producing content at volume.

**Second — the skill gap is real.** Knowing how to use a camera and knowing how to direct a piece of content are different skills. Knowing how to edit and knowing how to tell a story in edit are different skills. Staffing for the tool and hoping the craft follows is a common mistake.

## What Actually Works

In-house studios that succeed tend to have one of two things: a genuinely skilled editorial team with real production chops, or a clear lane that keeps them away from the work that requires those chops.

The mistake is blurring the line — asking an in-house team to produce work that requires a production company, at a pace and budget that only works for social-native content. You get the worst of both.

Figure out the lane first. Then build the team that fits it.
