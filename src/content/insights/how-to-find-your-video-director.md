You've got the concept. You spent weeks — maybe months — developing it. Now you need someone to make it real. Finding the right video director isn't just about finding a production company with a reel you like. It's about finding the specific person who will run your shoot.

Here's where most clients make the mistake.

## The Reel Is Not the Director

Production companies can have multiple directors. The spot you loved in their sizzle reel may have been shot by someone who's no longer on the roster, or by someone who specializes in a different type of content than what you're making.

Before you sign anything: ask who specifically would be directing your project, ask to see their individual reel, and confirm they'll be on set — not delegated to a second unit or an associate director.

## Three Questions Worth Asking

**Can they communicate their vision?** A good director can walk you through how they see the shoot before a camera turns on — point of view, look and feel, how a particular scene should move. If they can't articulate it, they can't execute it consistently.

**Do they understand your audience?** A director who's great at automotive work and a director who's great at healthcare content are solving different problems. Make sure the person you're hiring has done work for audiences that resemble yours.

**What happens when the plan changes on set?** Directors get tested by the unexpected — the location that doesn't work, the talent who can't deliver, the light that disappears. Ask them about a shoot that went sideways and what they did. The answer tells you more than the reel does.

## What a Great Director Actually Brings

Beyond technical execution, the best directors bring editorial instinct. They know when a take is emotionally true versus technically correct. They make decisions quickly under pressure. They protect the creative while staying practical about the schedule.

That combination — vision, communication, and operational judgment — is what separates a director you fall in love with from one you just survive working with.

Find the person, not just the company.
